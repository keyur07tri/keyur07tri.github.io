01 · Early Life

Early Life & Family

Jennifer Leigh Selter was born on August 8, 1993, in Roslyn, a village on Long Island, New York, and grew up in a Jewish family. She attended Roslyn High School. She has an older sister, Stephanie Selter, who has also worked in social media and entrepreneurship. Little is publicly known about her parents, and her college history is not documented, so this page does not guess.

In a Fitplan interview she described struggling with self-doubt and anxiety that began with bullying in middle school. She said she first turned to workouts because they lifted her mood, and the physical results came second. That mental-health angle still shapes how she talks about fitness today.

She started working out to feel better, and the physique followed. The mindset came first.

After high school she held several modest jobs, including a front-desk position at a gym, where her interest in training grew. Women's Health later reported that she left that job once her Instagram following reached roughly half a million.

02 · Career

Career Timeline

Jen Selter began posting gym photos on Instagram in 2012, when the app was still young and influencer marketing barely existed. Her glute-focused workouts and mirror shots spread fast, and she became widely credited with popularizing the belfie, earning the nickname Belfie Queen from the press. By early 2014 she had well over a million followers, and reports that year described a following in the millions.

Business followed quickly. In January 2014 she signed with The Legacy Agency, which pitched her as the next Jillian Michaels, and her first product endorsement was for CIRRUS fitness products. In October 2014 Premier Management Group took over her management, planning apparel, technology and food partnerships and a larger role with sponsor MusclePharm. Aggregator sites also list ambassador or apparel deals with Reebok in 2015 and Lorna Jane in 2016, though we could not confirm those with primary sources.

She later built her own training products through the Fitplan app and her website, appeared on TV shows including The View, Beyond the Tank and Live with Kelly and Mark, and became the face of GUESS's Spring 2021 activewear campaign. Press material also lists a partnership with Alo Yoga.

04 · Public Attention

Rumors, OnlyFans & Public Attention

Her fast rise brought plenty of criticism. In 2014 Women's Health noted that she did not hold a fitness certification at the time, and some fitness writers questioned her qualifications. Others argued that the belfie trend promoted an unrealistic body ideal. She has also been reported to have had cosmetic nose surgery as a teenager, which was covered in a People magazine feature.

06 · Fitness

Jen Selter Fitness & Mindset

Jen Selter's fitness message is built around consistency and self-talk. Her Fitplan interview stresses staying consistent even when results are slow, avoiding comparison with other bodies, facing difficult emotions, and replacing words like I can't with more supportive thoughts. She also suggests journaling and recalling past wins to rebuild confidence.

For people nervous about the gym, her advice is practical: follow a program, write down your workout before you go, bring a gym partner if it helps, avoid peak hours, and play music that motivates you. Reports say she trains about six days a week using bodyweight moves, resistance bands and dumbbells, with the stair machine and short HIIT blocks for cardio.

08 · Glute Workout

Jen Selter Glute & Leg Exercises

Lower-body training is the core of her brand. The moves most often linked to her style use little equipment, which makes them easy to do at home or in a gym.

  • Reverse lunges and reverse lunges with leg lifts
  • Banded squats
  • Squat with a pulse at the bottom
  • Squat into an overhead press
  • Curtsy lunges
  • Side lunges
  • Alternating jumping lunges (skip these if you have knee issues)

A simple way to use them is a circuit of 3 rounds with 12 to 15 reps per move and 30 to 45 seconds of rest. Focus on pressing through the heels, keeping the knees tracking over the toes and squeezing the glutes at the top of each rep.
